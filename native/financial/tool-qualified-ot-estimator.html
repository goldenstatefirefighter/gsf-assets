How much of your OT actually qualifies

The "no tax on overtime" deduction (IRC Section 225) only counts the premium half of overtime the FLSA itself requires, and fire protection runs under the special 7(k) rules. Run your numbers and see what actually qualifies.

Contract or MOU overtime below the applicable FLSA 7(k) ceiling does not qualify. A paystub label alone is not enough to identify qualified hours, so confirm the count with payroll.

The federal deduction is temporary for tax years 2025 through 2028 under current law. This calculator uses 2026 rules and reporting.

This route assumes a nonexempt employee engaged in fire protection activities and a valid employer-adopted 7(k) work period of 7 through 28 days. If your employer uses ordinary 40-hour FLSA overtime or your exemption/activity status differs, this route does not apply. Confirm with payroll before using these thresholds.

This is not always base hourly pay. The FLSA regular rate can include other remuneration. Use the rate payroll applies to the affected work periods.

Ask payroll for the hours above your federal 7(k) ceiling that required an FLSA overtime premium. Count a qualifying hour even if it was paid at double time; this tool applies only the potentially qualified minimum half-rate premium. Contract or MOU overtime below the ceiling does not count.
