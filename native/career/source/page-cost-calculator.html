Firefighter Cost and Timeline Calculator

Compare published costs for four possible preparation steps. Follow the requirements for your target role; every department does not require all four. The tool shows a named-program subtotal and an illustrative preparation-time estimate.

How it works

Tap what you have finished. See what is left.

The calculator lists four steps in the order most California candidates take them. Tap each one you have already completed. It returns three things: an estimated cost range, an estimated prep time range, and a plain description of the first step you have not finished. Finish all four and the cost reads $0.

Everything comes back as a range when the named programs differ. The calculator compares published totals from specific California schools. It does not treat those examples as a statewide market range.

Nothing you tap is saved or sent anywhere. No email field, no account. Reload the page and it has forgotten you.

The data

Every figure has a source

The figures below were checked in September 2026 against published fee pages. Fees change, so confirm the current price and inclusions with the program before you enroll.

EMT certification

$800 to $1,475, and 2 to 4 months. Palomar College publishes an estimated $800 course total. Ventura College lists $1,163 for its program plus $104 for NREMT, $75 for Live Scan, and $133 for California certification, or $1,475 from the listed items. These are named examples, and the schools may define their totals differently.

Fire academy or Firefighter I

$4,000 to $7,929, and an illustrative 3 to 6 months. El Camino College publishes a $4,000 to $5,000 academy estimate. South Bay Regional Public Safety Training Consortium publishes $7,730 for academy, PPE and uniform plus a mandatory $199 preparation class. Its $7,929 subtotal excludes the conditional 3% Visa/Mastercard fee. The listed part-time October 2026 through March 2027 academy is about six months; full-time schedules differ. El Camino lists $3,782 to $3,982 in itemized components but publishes an approximate $4,000 to $5,000 planning total. The calculator uses that complete $4,000 published estimate and South Bay's $7,929 subtotal.

CPAT

$160, and 1 to 2 months to train for and schedule. That is a single published figure rather than a range, so the tool shows it as one number instead of inventing a spread around it.

FCTC written test and eligibility

$50, and 0 to 1 month. Figure drawn from the FCTC fee and scholarship page, linked inside the tool.

If you have finished none of them

The tool returns a named-example subtotal of $5,010 to $9,614, and an illustrative 6 to 13 months of preparation. The low combines Palomar EMT, El Camino academy, FCTC written, and CPAT. The high combines Ventura EMT with its listed external certification fees, South Bay academy, FCTC written, and CPAT. It does not claim either total is typical statewide.

The $800 Palomar example is its published course total; confirm outside exam, Live Scan and local certification costs separately. South Bay's mandatory $199 preparation course is included above. Its conditional 3% Visa/Mastercard charge remains excluded. Resident tuition and program eligibility must be confirmed with each provider. Preparation times are planning estimates, not published hiring deadlines or a prediction of when you will be hired.

Straight talk

What this calculator does not tell you

This is an estimate, not a quote, and it is deliberately narrow. Here is what it leaves out.

  • Paramedic school. Not one of the four steps, and its cost is nowhere in the total. If your target departments hire mostly paramedics, your real number is considerably higher.
  • Living costs and lost wages. Most academies run full time. The income you give up while attending is often larger than the tuition.
  • Retests. A failed written or physical test costs the fee again, plus the wait for the next date.
  • Department expenses. Application fees, medical exams, uniforms, and relocation vary and are not counted.
  • The wait. The months figure covers preparation only. It does not estimate how long a hiring window takes to open, or how long you sit on an eligibility list. For most candidates that wait is the longest part of the climb, and no calculator can predict it.
  • Your odds. Nothing here scores how competitive you are. Cost and time are not the same thing as chances.

These four steps are common, not universal. Some departments accept applications before you hold an EMT. Some run their own written exam instead of the FCTC test. Some use the Biddle, BPAT, or VCPAT physical instead of the CPAT. Read the current bulletin for every department you are targeting before you spend money on a step it does not ask for.
