California Fire Department Directory

California has hundreds of fire agencies and no single place that says what each one wants from an applicant. This directory covers the career departments in our current statewide dataset, with contact details, requirement evidence, check dates, and a plain flag where the public record does not establish an answer.

How to use it

Filter first, then read the notes

Search by department, city, county or fire chief, narrow to one of nine regions or any of 49 counties, or press a filter chip to show only departments on the FCTC statewide list, or only those known to require an EMT to apply. The count updates as you filter.

Every card carries the region, the agency type, the tags, a hiring pattern where we could establish one, notes, and a link to the department's careers page. Read the notes. That is where the exceptions live, and exceptions cost cycles.

What is in here

The current statewide dataset

Coverage comes from the State Fire Marshal roster and includes city departments, fire protection districts, county departments, fire authorities, and CAL FIRE. Contract-served agencies stay in the directory because candidates need to know which employer actually hires for them.

The live summary above derives every FCTC and EMT yes, no, and not-confirmed total from the same hosted array used by the filters. A roster absence is not converted into a confirmed no. Where a field says not confirmed, the public evidence did not establish either answer.

How this was built

Where the data comes from

Every record here was compiled from official department material and public rosters, one agency at a time. It is not a purchased list. Each card shows the recorded requirement evidence and check date when available, plus the FCTC basis and its roster check date. A missing source is labelled instead of being filled with a guess.

The directory is a reviewed snapshot, not a live feed, and a department's current job bulletin always outranks anything on this page. Confirm on their posting before you rely on it. Our full source hierarchy, inclusion rules, review-date policy, and correction route are in our editorial standards. If a record is wrong, tell us at hello@goldenstatefirefighter.com and we will check it against the department's own material and fix every page carrying the same claim.

Why some fields say unknown

An honest blank beats a confident guess

Some cards carry a tag reading FCTC list: not confirmed, EMT to apply: not confirmed, or Paramedic: not confirmed. Those are evidence states, not negative answers.

Unconfirmed requirement tags

Requirements often live inside a recruitment bulletin and disappear between cycles. When the reviewed public material did not establish yes or no, the card says not confirmed. Treat that as a question to resolve from the next bulletin, never as permission to assume no.

Hiring pattern not published

Some departments do not publish a recurring cycle. Those cards say the hiring pattern is not published instead of supplying a season. Set up an official job alert and check the GovernmentJobs sample on this site.

The same discipline runs through every note. Where a bulletin hedges, the note hedges with it. This directory would look more authoritative with every cell filled, and it would be lying.

Straight talk

What this directory does not tell you

  • 46 of these do not employ their own firefighters. They are served under contract, usually by CAL FIRE or a county department. Those cards name the real employer, because applying to the wrong one wastes an application.
  • It is not a live job board. No card says whether a recruitment is open now. Follow the careers link for that.
  • It is a reviewed snapshot. The hosted dataset can be refreshed, but department requirements still change between cycles. Each card shows its recorded evidence and check date.
  • No pay, benefits, or pension tier. Those vary by bargaining unit and hire date and go stale fast.
  • It does not rank departments. No best-to-worst order and no opinion on which to target. That depends on where you can live and what you can qualify for.
  • It does not estimate your chances. Meeting every listed minimum makes you eligible, not competitive.

One naming trap worth knowing before you search. Several California departments share a city name with a separate agency, and applying to the wrong one wastes a cycle. LAFD and the Los Angeles County Fire Department are different employers with different tests. So are Santa Clara County Fire and the City of Santa Clara, Santa Barbara City and County, and Bakersfield City and Kern County. For contract-served cities, confirm the current hiring authority rather than assuming the local service name is a separate employer.
